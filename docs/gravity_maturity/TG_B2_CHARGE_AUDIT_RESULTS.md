# TG-B2 P1 Charge Audit — Why is the Force Mass-Flat? (it's NOT the source normalization)

## Result — the prior causal claim is falsified

**Scaling exponents vs node mass M (fit `X ~ M^p` across the w-family, mass 38.7→105.9):**

| quantity | exponent p | reading |
|---|---:|---|
| measured force `<F_R_well>` | **−0.07** | flat (the M⁰ under investigation) |
| raw extensive load `∫E_dens` | +0.94 | properly extensive (~M¹) |
| U(1) charge `Q` | +0.97 | extensive (~M¹) |
| normalization maxima `e_ref`, `q_ref` | +0.36 | per-w recomputed; scale sub-linearly |
| **normalized source charge `∫S_state`** | **+0.59** | **STILL SCALES — not flattened to zero** |
| **receiver weight `∫\|∇φ\|²`** | **+1.10** | scales ~linearly with mass |
| geometric well `∫(1−A)` | +0.59 | tracks the source charge |

**The naive factorization `F ~ Q_source · Q_receiver` predicts `M^(0.59+1.10) = M^1.69`. The force is `M^−0.07`.**
So there is a **~1.7-power cancellation** between the (still-scaling) charges and the near-field kernel.

## What this means (corrected diagnosis)

1. **The "M⁰ = source-normalization artifact" story is wrong / incomplete.** Peak normalization *does* remove *some*
   source scaling (raw load `M^0.94` → normalized source `M^0.59`, ≈0.35 powers, via `e_ref`/`q_ref ~ M^0.36`), and
   the normalized profile broadens at low mass (`source_rms_width` 2.85→3.55) — exactly the reviews' caveat that
   peak-normalization does *not* guarantee a constant integrated charge. **But the source charge still scales**, so
   normalization does **not** explain the flat force.
2. **The mass-flatness is a NEAR-FIELD KERNEL effect at sep=3.0.** `F_R` is not a product of monopole charges — it is
   the overlap integral `−c²∫_{x>0} ∂_xA·|∇φ|² dV` in the region *between* the nodes (distance ~1.5 from each centre,
   comparable to node size). As mass grows, the global charges grow but the overlap-region field structure happens to
   compensate, cancelling ~1.7 powers of M. This is the "near-field convolution dominates over point-charge scaling"
   regime the external-comparison review flagged.
3. **Therefore the mass-independence is SEPARATION-SPECIFIC, not intrinsic.** The near-field `M⁰` at sep=3.0 is *not*
   the interaction's true mass-scaling. A far-field mass sweep (separation ≫ node size), where the monopole picture
   applies, could give a *different* (non-zero) exponent. The mass-scaling question cannot be answered in the near
   field.

## F_R classification (audit requirement)

`F_R_well = −c²∫_{x>0} (∂_xA) |∇φ|² dV` is a **total (half-space) body FORCE** — not an acceleration, not
mass-normalized, no mass/volume division inside it. So a fixed-source `M⁰` in *force* would imply *acceleration* ~
`1/M` (not universal free fall). The `M⁰` here is in force, and is anyway near-field-specific (point 3).

## Verdict and next step

```text
Verdict: SOURCE_SCALES_BUT_FORCE_FLAT — near-field kernel cancellation, NOT source-normalization
```

This redirects the mass question exactly as the reviews' Gate 3 prescribes: **do a measured-kernel convolution +
far-field extension**, not another near-field pair fit. Concretely:
- **Far-field mass sweep**: repeat F(mass) at separation ≫ node size (large box) — does an exponent emerge?
- **Kernel convolution**: measure the single-source T/G→A response kernel, convolve with the measured source and
  receiver profiles, and predict F(sep, M) with no free exponent — does it reproduce the near-field flatness *and* a
  non-trivial far-field mass-scaling?
- The **asymmetric source/probe sweep** (Gate 2, `F ∝ M_s^α M_p^β`) still separates active source from passive probe;
  with this audit it should be done in the *far field* to escape the near-field cancellation.
