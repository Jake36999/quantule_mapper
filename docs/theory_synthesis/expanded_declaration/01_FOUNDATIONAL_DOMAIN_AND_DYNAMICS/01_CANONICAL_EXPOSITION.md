# Canonical Exposition

Status: self-contained reader-facing explanation written after the preservation documents. This document explains the AIS/PIF -> Informational Indifference -> gradients -> Axis of Least Effort -> Informational Parallels -> FMIA family for a new collaborator who cannot open v9, the raw transcripts, or the Phase 1 ledger.

## The Problem This Family Tries To Solve

IRER begins from a question about how a stable, apparently physical reality can emerge from something more fundamental than ordinary matter in ordinary spacetime. The foundational domain family asks:

- What is the underlying domain from which structure, locality, time, and force-like behaviour emerge?
- If this domain is informational, why does anything happen at all?
- What makes a configuration move toward stability rather than remain undifferentiated potential?
- Why do systems seem to follow lawful paths?
- How can "force" be treated as emergent rather than primitive?

The family answers with a chain:

AIS/PIF is the proposed informational domain. Informational or Potentiality Indifference is the baseline tendency of that domain. Deviations from indifference create gradients. Gradients create directionality. Directionality makes some paths lower cost or more coherent than others. Jake's early phrases for those preferred pathways include "parralel of probabilities," "path of least activity," "axis of least effort," and "informational parralel." The mature v9 name for the principle is FMIA: Fields of Minimal Informational Action.

That chain is the backbone. Everything else in this family is either an elaboration, formal translation, application, or unresolved branch.

## AIS: A-temporal Informational Substrate

AIS is the v9 name for the most basic IRER domain: a pre-spacetime, ontologically real field of informational potential from which all structure emerges. In v9, Jake distinguishes AIS from a passive background. It is not a container waiting for particles. It is the proposed generative stratum from which time, causality, material structure, and field behaviour arise.

The key feature of AIS is that it contains varying informational potentials. Where those potentials are not indifferent or balanced, they form gradients. Those gradients then become the active drivers of resonance, pathing, collapse, and force-like behaviour.

To explain AIS without relying on "field" as a vague analogy: imagine the pre-physical condition as a space of possible informational relations. A relation can remain unresolved, or it can become differentiated by resonance, entropy, alignment, tension, or potential for locality. AIS is the proposed domain in which those differences can exist before they become ordinary spatial differences. It is "a-temporal" because the ordering of events is not assumed at the start; ordering emerges later through resolution.

AIS remains an IRER-native ontology claim, not a proven physical entity. In current technical language it may be represented by grids, fields, order parameters, or process spaces, but those are implementation proxies or analogies.

## PIF: The Unresolved Partner Term

PIF appears in later dialogue and summaries as Primordial Informational Field or Potential Information Field. It often appears in AI responses and later project summaries where the discussion is implementation-facing or IQG-facing. The archive does not resolve whether PIF is:

- a synonym for AIS;
- a later replacement for AIS;
- the active process layer of AIS;
- an IQG/S-NCGL modelling term;
- an AI-introduced or AI-stabilized editorial label.

The safest current reading is: AIS is the stronger original declaration term for the foundational domain; PIF is a later or parallel process-field framing. A new collaborator should not silently merge them. They should say: "AIS/PIF is the proposed pre-geometric informational domain, but the exact semantic relationship between AIS and PIF remains unresolved."

## Informational Indifference

Informational Indifference is the proposed tendency of the informational domain toward an equilibrium-like or unresolved state. The term does not mean the substrate has no dynamics. It means the baseline is one of no resolved difference until a relation, tension, or opportunity creates a departure from that neutrality.

Jake's May 18 wording is important because it shows the concept in motion rather than as a polished definition. He says there may not be "definitive rules," but a constant "goal" of everything. He says Informational Indifference may be better described as Potentiality Indifference. He connects this to equilibrium, field influence, light, informational parallels, angles of the manifold, locality, resonance, and entropic potential.

In current formal writing, "goal" and "desire" should not be treated as literal agency unless Jake explicitly chooses that metaphysical reading. The physics-facing translation is tendency, relaxation, minimization, or unresolved potential. The historical wording is still preserved because it explains why the concept originally felt like a law without being an externally imposed rule.

## Potentiality Indifference

Potentiality Indifference appears in Jake's May 18 prompt as a possible better phrase. It may indicate that the relevant baseline is not only informational neutrality but unresolved potential: the state before locality, resonance, or entropic direction becomes selected.

There are three possible interpretations:

1. Potentiality Indifference is an alternate name for Informational Indifference.
2. Potentiality Indifference names the pre-gradient state, while Informational Indifference names the broader principle.
3. Potentiality Indifference is an abandoned or transient wording.

The archive does not choose between these. This is a Jake review item.

## From Indifference To Gradients

The transition from indifference to gradient is the first core mechanism. In v9, the claim is direct: deviations from a baseline of complete indifference or equilibrium create informational gradients.

A gradient is a difference that has direction. In IRER terms, that difference might involve:

- informational potential;
- resonance density;
- actualization readiness;
- entropy or decoherence load;
- phase alignment;
- Payan spin-axis alignment;
- manifold curvature or angular mismatch;
- topology/path tension.

The important point is that the gradient is not merely descriptive. It is proposed as generative. It is what gives a system a direction to resolve, stabilize, collapse, move, align, or decohere.

The AI phase-field translation maps this to standard language: free-energy gradients, entropy gradients, chemical potential, gradient descent, and torque. Those mappings help the reader recognize the structure. They do not prove that IRER has derived its own equations.

## Path Selection: From Probability Parallels To Axis Of Least Effort

The path-selection vocabulary developed in stages.

On May 17, Jake asks whether electron-neutron pairs may align to their most stable manifold "angle" across a "parralel of probabilities" to reduce entropy caused by delocalisation or uncoupling. This wording is raw but conceptually rich. It already contains the idea that a manifold has preferred angular relations and that stable pairings follow paths that reduce entropy.

In the same conversation Jake then asks the AI to consolidate this with resonant field convergences taking the "path of least activity." He describes fields aligning themselves or pulling others into a stable resonant field to avoid "disappearing," creating deterministic behaviour that can be observed and translated. He also adds spin and rotational-energy conservation as necessary for linking micro structure to macro structure.

On May 18, Jake supplies "axis of least effort" and "informational parralel." He uses them in a sentence about "coupled rotational information indifferences" maintaining energy conservation as they move along that axis within the informational parallel.

These are not separate finished theories. They are developmental names for the same path-selection intuition:

- stable configurations are not arbitrary;
- a field or structure tends to preserve coherence;
- coherence can require a lower-effort route;
- that route may be constrained by manifold topology or angular alignment;
- following the route reduces decoherence, entropy, or dissipation;
- stable repeated routes become lawful-looking behaviour.

## Informational Parallels

Informational Parallels are the proposed path structures through which this least-effort behaviour occurs. Historically, the wording appears as "parallel of probabilities," "parallels of probability," "informational parralel," and later "Informational Parallels" or "Coherent Manifold Channels."

The term should not be understood as a literal parallel line in Euclidean space. It is closer to a coherence channel: a pathway through the informational manifold where dissipation, decoherence, or resistance is lower. Informational Parallels may be defined by resonance continuity, manifold angle, topology, Payan alignment, or local gradient structure.

Open issue: the archive does not yet prove whether "probability parallels" and "Informational Parallels" are synonyms or whether the former was an early probabilistic phrasing later replaced by a structural/path phrasing.

## FMIA: Fields Of Minimal Informational Action

FMIA is the mature v9 name for the path-selection principle. It says that informational systems tend to move, evolve, resolve, or configure along pathways that minimize informational action or effort.

In standard physics, least-action principles describe paths that make an action functional stationary. FMIA is not simply the standard least-action principle pasted onto IRER. The IRER claim is deeper and more speculative: in an informational substrate, the least-action-like path is an emergent expression of Informational Indifference and gradient resolution.

The formal status must be separated into layers:

- FMIA as IRER-native concept: central principle of minimal informational action/pathing.
- FMIA as historical development: built from Jake's "path of least activity," "axis of least effort," and "informational parralel" language.
- FMIA as AI-assisted formal packaging: phase-field/free-energy/geodesic/least-action mapping supplied by AI.
- FMIA as proposed mathematical formalism: placeholders like S_IRER action integrals or Lagrangian terms.
- FMIA as implementation proxy: old FMIA-labelled code/proxy structures.
- FMIA Stable Proxy as superseded implementation: later report says the old FMIA proxy was obsolete relative to S-NCGL-native worker logic.

## What A New Collaborator Should Be Able To Say

IRER's foundational domain family proposes that reality emerges from an a-temporal informational domain. In Jake's v9 language this is AIS; later summaries also use PIF, but their relation remains unresolved. The domain tends toward indifference or unresolved potential. When differences arise, they form gradients. Those gradients create directionality. Directionality makes coherent, low-dissipation pathways possible. Jake first expressed these through probability parallels, paths of least activity, axes of least effort, and informational parallels. The mature declaration term is FMIA, the principle that systems follow paths of minimal informational action. AI helped translate this into phase-field/free-energy/geodesic/force analogies, but those analogies are not concept origination and are not yet first-principles derivations.
