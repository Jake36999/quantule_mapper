## Ambiguous Or Mixed Attribution

Several passages are useful but cannot be cleanly attributed:

- `Aleheia'sChat.txt` recaps may summarize prior conversations, uploaded documents, or the AI's reconstruction. They are
  good continuity evidence but weak first-appearance evidence.
- "Process, not stuff" appears in an AI-generated ontology map. It may capture Jake's stance, but unless earlier Jake
  wording is found, it should be treated as AI editorial phrasing.
- Poetic lines about the substrate "desiring" indifference are historically useful but may be AI-styled language and are
  not formal theory statements.
- November/December report language may be AI-authored from project artifacts. It is strong as a status/revision record
  only when backed by code/run artifacts in a later evidence pass.

## Attribution Rules For The Updated Declaration

Use these distinctions:

- Jake originated the intuitive ontology and vocabulary where the transcript shows Jake saying it or the declaration
  explicitly self-attributes it.
- AI translated concepts into standard formalisms where equations, phase-field language, Lagrangian terms, or report
  structures appear in AI responses.
- AI recaps do not prove first conception.
- Simulation results do not prove the ontology; they can only support implementation status, analogical fit, or later
  testing pathways.
- Relational/Aletheia language should be separated from physics claims unless it directly explains project method or
  provenance.

## Primary Risk

The primary risk is not that AI created the concepts wholesale. The located May 18 source strongly shows Jake providing
the core vocabulary before the AI formalizes it. The risk is subtler: later documents smooth Jake's raw phrasing into
standard physics language, and those polished forms can obscure which parts were original intuition, AI translation,
speculation, or later implementation status.
