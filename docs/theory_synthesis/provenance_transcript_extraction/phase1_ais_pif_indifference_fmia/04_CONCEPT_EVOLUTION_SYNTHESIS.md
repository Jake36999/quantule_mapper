# Concept Evolution Synthesis

This synthesis follows the ledger. It should not be read without `01_PASSAGE_LEDGER.md`.

## 1. AIS/PIF: From Substrate To Process Domain

The declaration defines AIS as a pre-spacetime informational substrate. Later summaries often pair or substitute PIF,
especially in IQG/S-NCGL contexts. The stable conceptual core is not the label but the role:

- it is prior to ordinary spacetime description;
- it is generative rather than passive;
- it contains or produces differences in informational potential;
- those differences become gradients;
- gradients drive resonance, collapse, and path selection.

Current wording should avoid saying AIS/PIF is proven. A safer formulation is:

IRER describes its fundamental domain as AIS/PIF: a proposed pre-geometric informational field/process space. In Jake's
original terminology, deviations from informational indifference generate gradients. AI-assisted formal work later mapped
this onto field-theoretic and variational analogues.

## 2. Informational Indifference: From Equilibrium Intuition To Gradient Generator

The earliest located direct phrasing comes from the May 18 phase-field discussion. Jake connects informational or
potentiality indifference with equilibrium and with opportunities to change locality when resonance or entropic potential
differs. The declaration later upgrades this to a foundational principle: deviations from a baseline of indifference
create gradients.

Evolution:

1. Raw intuition: the field has a tendency toward indifference/equilibrium.
2. Local difference: deviations from indifference create tension, resonance opportunity, or entropic potential.
3. Gradient mechanism: those deviations become informational gradients.
4. Formal analogue: AI maps this to free-energy/entropy gradients and least-action style minimization.
5. Formalism gap: November 2025 clarifies that deriving the active equation from this principle remains unfinished.

Error/risk preserved: teleological language such as "desires perfect indifference" appears in the root bridge transcript.
That language should be retained as historical/philosophical context but avoided in final physics claims.

## 3. Gradients And Forces: From Path Selection To Emergent Force Analogues

The May 17 and May 18 transcripts show the bridge from intuitive paths to force analogues. Jake asks whether the
phase-field description gives "forces of an IRER field." AI responds using standard physics mappings:

- force as negative gradient of a potential/free-energy-like term;
- entropy gradient contribution;
- torque from spin/Payan misalignment;
- geodesic or least-resistance motion.

The declaration later records this as Gradient-Derived Informational Forces. The mature version includes many extensions:
force memory, hysteresis, propagation, boundary effects, quantization hypotheses, gauge analogies, and force analogues.

Current status:

- useful as a conceptual framework;
- useful as a source of simulation semantics;
- not a demonstrated unification of physical forces;
- not evidence of gravity or matter proof;
- not yet fully axiom-derived if the S-NCGL formalism remains an imported analogue.

## 4. FMIA: From "Path Of Least Activity" To Obsolete Proxy And Continuing Principle

FMIA evolves through several layers:

1. May 17: "path of least activity" and entropy-reducing parallels.
2. May 18: "axis of least effort" and "informational parralel" in Jake's phase-field prompt.
3. May 18 AI response: least informational action, free-energy minimization, coherent parallels, geodesics.
4. Declaration: FMIA becomes the named principle of minimal informational action, incorporating Axis of Least Effort and
   Informational Parallels.
5. Root bridge status: simulations suggest only partial/implicit FMIA behavior and are not solving a variational problem.
6. December: the implementation called "FMIA Stable Proxy" is explicitly obsolete relative to S-NCGL-native worker logic.

Therefore the updated declaration should distinguish:

- FMIA as a user-originated conceptual principle;
- FMIA as AI-assisted formal naming/translation;
- FMIA as a proposed variational/minimization formalism;
- FMIA Stable Proxy as a historical implementation that has been superseded.
